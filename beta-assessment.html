Supply Chain Builder — Guide
Everything you need to know to build your supply chain correctly
💡 What is a supply chain, and why does it matter?
✦ How Available and Partial work in this assessment
✅ Available
You have the document and can produce it on demand. To mark Available, upload your own copy in the + Add New tab — a PDF, photo, certificate or URL to your own records.
◑ Partial
Manhattan's database has a record of this data — but you haven't yet shown that your company holds the documentation itself. This means the data exists somewhere in the supply chain, but your own records may not have it.

To upgrade Partial → Available: click the amber note on any question, then upload your own copy of the document.
✗ Not Available
This data doesn't exist or cannot be produced. These are your genuine traceability gaps — the things SmarTuna will help you build towards over time.
💡 The gap between Partial and Available is the gap report. A company scoring 80/100 on Partial but 40/100 on Available has the data in the supply chain — they just don't hold it themselves yet. That's exactly what SmarTuna helps fix.
A supply chain is the journey your seafood product takes from the ocean (or farm) to the buyer. Every step in that journey is a node. You need to map it so SmarTuna can ask the right traceability questions at each step — and so auditors can see exactly where the product came from, who handled it, and what checks were done along the way.

Think of it like drawing a flowchart. Each box in the chart is a node. You connect them left to right in the order things happened. SmarTuna then generates a tailored assessment based on your specific chain.
✦ How to build your chain — step by step
1Pick your nodes from the left panel. Look at the list and find the ones that match what actually happened to your product. You don't need to use all of them — just the ones that apply.
2Drag them onto the canvas in the order they happened — left to right. The first event goes on the left, the last event on the right.
3Connect the nodes. Drag from the ● dot on the right side of one node to the ● dot on the left side of the next. This tells the system that one step flows into the next.
4Click any node on the canvas to give it a name, set the number of vessels, or add other details. The system needs this to generate the right questions.
5Click Save Chain → when you're happy with it. SmarTuna will then build your assessment based on exactly what you mapped.
⚠ Important — Multiple vessels
If you had 3 fishing vessels on the same trip, catching the same species in the same area — you add ONE Harvest/Catch node and set the vessel count to 3 inside it. SmarTuna will ask you to verify all 3 vessels together.

Only add two separate Harvest nodes if the vessels fished completely independently — for example, one in the WCPFC area and another in the IOTC area, catching for different lots. If they were working the same fishing trip together, they belong in one node.
🌊 Wild Catch Nodes
🎣
Harvest / Catch
The act of catching fish at sea.
This is where your supply chain starts for wild-caught seafood. It represents the fishing event — the boat going out, the nets going in, and fish coming up. Add one node for each group of fishing activity that produced the same lot of product. You'll set the vessel count, FAO fishing area, catch dates, and species inside this node. SmarTuna will then cross-reference your vessels against RFMO registers, IUU blacklists, and flag state records automatically.
Example: Your company bought tuna from a fleet of 5 purse seiners that fished together in the Western Pacific. → Add 1 Harvest/Catch node, set vessel count to 5.
🔪
On-Vessel Processing
Processing that happens on the fishing boat itself, while still at sea.
Some fishing vessels don't just catch fish — they also process it on board before the product is offloaded. Common examples include heading and gutting (H&G), filleting, blast freezing, or mincing. If any of this happened on your fishing vessel before the product was transferred or landed, add this node after Harvest/Catch and connect them.
When to skip it: If the fish went straight from the net into the hold and wasn't touched until it reached port, skip this node. Also skip it if the transformation was simply live fish to frozen (whole round) and you don't have enough evidence to prove the processing happened on board — omitting this step means fewer questions to answer, and the processing can be captured in a land-based Processing node instead.
🔄
Transshipment
When catch is moved from the fishing vessel to a carrier or reefer vessel.
Transshipment is a high-risk step in the supply chain because it's where IUU fish can enter a legitimate shipment. It happens when the fishing vessel meets a refrigerated carrier vessel (reefer) at sea or in port and offloads its catch. The carrier then takes the fish to port. If your supply chain includes this step, you must add this node — SmarTuna will ask for the carrier vessel details, whether it was an authorised at-sea transshipment, and for the RFMO authorisation letter.
Note: Each separate transshipment event gets its own node — even if they happened on the same trip. If Vessel A transshipped to Carrier 1 and then to Carrier 2, that's 2 Transshipment nodes.
⚓
Landing / Discharge
When fish is unloaded at a port from a carrier vessel or container.
This node represents the moment product arrives at a port and is discharged from the vessel. Use this when the product came in on a carrier vessel (not the original fishing boat). SmarTuna will ask for the port name, whether it's a PSMA-designated port, the landing declaration, and EU approval status where relevant.
Typical flow: Harvest → Transshipment → Landing / Discharge → Processing
🛳️
Direct Vessel Landing
When the fishing boat itself goes directly to port and unloads there.
Simpler than Landing/Discharge — use this when there was no carrier vessel involved. The fishing vessel sailed straight to port, docked, and the catch was unloaded directly. This is common in small-scale or coastal fisheries. SmarTuna will still ask for the port, landing records, and any applicable documentation, but won't require carrier vessel details.
Typical flow: Harvest → Direct Vessel Landing → Processing
🌾 Aquaculture Nodes
🌊
Farm / Grow-out
The farm where fish or seafood is raised to harvest size.
This is the starting node for aquaculture supply chains. It represents the facility where the species is grown — whether it's a salmon cage in Norway, a shrimp pond in Vietnam, or a tilapia tank in Egypt. SmarTuna will ask for the farm's certifications (ASC, BAP, GLOBALG.A.P), welfare standards, feed sourcing, and environmental compliance. Most aquaculture chains start here.
Typical flow: Farm / Grow-out → Processing → Ship / Receive
🐣
Hatchery
Where juvenile fish (fry or fingerlings) are produced before going to the farm.
A hatchery breeds fish and produces juveniles that are then sent to a grow-out farm. You only need to include this node if your audit scope goes back that far — for example, if you're assessing a vertically integrated operation that controls its own broodstock, or if your buyer requires traceability to the hatchery level. SmarTuna will ask for registration, disease management records, and broodstock sourcing.
When to include it: Only if you own or source from a hatchery and your scope covers it. If you just buy fish from a farm and the hatchery is the farm's supplier, you may not need this.
🌾
Feedmill
Where fish feed is produced — typically using fishmeal and fish oil from wild-caught fish.
Feed is one of the most complex parts of an aquaculture supply chain because it often contains marine ingredients (fishmeal, fish oil) sourced from wild capture fisheries. If your assessment scope includes feed ingredient sourcing — for example, for IFFO RS or MarinTrust compliance — include this node. It sits upstream of the hatchery or farm. SmarTuna will ask about the feed mill's certifications and the origin of marine ingredients.
When to include it: Only if you need to demonstrate responsible sourcing of feed ingredients as part of your assessment. Most importers don't need to go this far back.
🔗 Shared Nodes — used in both Wild Catch and Aquaculture
📦
Aggregation / Disaggregation
When lots are combined into one batch, or one batch is split into smaller ones.
This is one of the trickiest nodes to get right. Aggregation is when product from multiple sources (different vessels, different farms, different fishing trips) is mixed together into a single lot. Disaggregation is when a large batch is split into smaller ones for different customers or destinations. Both must be documented carefully because they break the one-to-one link between the product and its origin. SmarTuna will ask for the input lot codes and output lot codes so the audit trail stays intact.
Example: A cold store receives tuna from 3 different vessels and mixes it into one container lot. → Add an Aggregation node between the Harvest nodes and the next step.
🏭
Processing
Any land-based facility where the product is transformed or prepared.
Processing covers everything from simple activities like weighing and packing, all the way to filleting, canning, smoking, cooking, mincing, or freezing. Add one Processing node per facility. If the product goes to Factory A and then to Factory B, that's two separate Processing nodes connected in sequence. SmarTuna will ask for the facility's food safety certifications (HACCP, BRC, FSSC 22000 etc), EU approval number, and welfare practices.
Don't confuse with On-Vessel Processing — that node is only for processing that happens on the fishing boat at sea. This Processing node is always land-based.
🚛
Ship / Receive
When the product is shipped out or officially received — the handover point.
This node represents any movement of product between parties or locations — it is not always the last node. Use it whenever product is shipped, transported, or transferred: export from country A to country B, domestic transport from Processor 1 to Processor 2, delivery to a cold store, or handover to an importer. It captures the chain of custody at every transfer point, which is how Manhattan can confirm there was no mixing or substitution between steps. You can have multiple Ship / Receive nodes in one chain. SmarTuna will ask for the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and any applicable import compliance documents (SIMP for US imports, IFTP for EU imports).
Example: Harvest → Processing (Thailand) → Ship/Receive → Processing (UK) → Ship/Receive → Ship/Receive (final delivery). Each handover gets its own node.
