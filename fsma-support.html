How FSMA 204 works

What is it?

FDA's Food Traceability Rule (Section 204 of the Food Safety Modernization Act) asks firms that manufacture, process, pack or hold foods on the Food Traceability List to keep extra records, so FDA can trace a food quickly during an outbreak or recall.

Is tuna covered?

Yes. The list names histamine-producing finfish, including tuna, in fresh, frozen and previously frozen form, and smoked finfish too.

When?

FDA will not enforce the rule before July 20, 2028. The original date was January 20, 2026.

The three ideas

Critical Tracking Events: the moments you must record, such as first land-based receiving, receiving, transformation and shipping.
Key Data Elements: the facts recorded at each event, such as the lot code, quantity, product description, the locations involved and the date.
Traceability Lot Code: the code that ties all of a lot's records together. You assign a new one only when you first receive fish from a vessel, transform a food, or receive from a firm that is exempt from the rule.

When FDA asks

Keep records for 2 years. During an outbreak or recall, FDA can ask for them as an electronic sortable spreadsheet within 24 hours. This tool builds that spreadsheet with the same tabs and columns as FDA's template.

How is this different from SIMP?

SIMP (NOAA) checks that imported fish was caught legally. FSMA 204 (FDA) is about food safety: tracing where a lot went, fast. Many details overlap, but they are separate records for separate agencies.
