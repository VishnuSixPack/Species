Traceability plan

Every firm covered by the rule must keep a written traceability plan (21 CFR 1.1315), keep it current, and keep each earlier version for 2 years after replacing it.

1. How you keep your records§ 1.1315(a)(1)

Describe the procedures, including the format and location of the records. You may keep them in more than one set of records, and another firm may keep them for you, as long as they can be produced within 24 hours.

2. How you identify foods on the list§ 1.1315(a)(2)

How you tell which of the foods you handle are on FDA's Food Traceability List, for example tuna that is fresh, frozen, previously frozen or smoked.

3. How you assign Traceability Lot Codes§ 1.1315(a)(3)

You assign a code only when you initially pack farmed fish, first receive fish on land from a vessel, transform a food, or receive from a supplier that is exempt from the rule. You must not assign a new code when you only ship.

FSMA 204, explained for tuna

01

The rule in one page

Section 204 of the FDA Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) told FDA to set extra record-keeping for high-risk foods. FDA's final rule, Requirements for Additional Traceability Records for Certain Foods, was published on 21 November 2022 (87 FR 70910). Firms that manufacture, process, pack or hold a food on the Food Traceability List must keep Key Data Elements (KDEs) for each Critical Tracking Event (CTE), linked to a Traceability Lot Code, and pass some of them to the next firm. The aim is to trace a food in hours, not weeks, during an outbreak or recall.

21 Nov 2022Final rule published
20 Jan 2023Rule took effect
20 Jan 2026Original compliance date
20 Jul 2028FDA won't enforce before this date

In August 2025 FDA proposed moving compliance by 30 months, and Congress then directed FDA not to enforce the rule before 20 July 2028. Some older guides, including vendor e-books, still say January 2026.

The rule covers domestic and foreign firms producing food for U.S. consumption. It doesn't require any particular technology: paper and electronic records both work, as long as you can produce them in time.

02

Is tuna covered?

Yes. Almost all seafood is on FDA's Food Traceability List. The seafood entries are:

On the listFormsExamples and notes
Finfish, histamine-producing speciesFresh, frozen, previously frozenTuna, mahi mahi, mackerel, amberjack, jack, swordfish, yellowtail
Finfish, species potentially contaminated with ciguatoxinFresh, frozen, previously frozenGrouper, barracuda, snapper
Finfish, other speciesFresh, frozen, previously frozenCod, haddock, Alaska pollock, salmon, tilapia, trout. Siluriformes (catfish) are not included.
Smoked finfishRefrigerated and frozenCold-smoked and hot-smoked
CrustaceansFresh and frozenShrimp, crab, lobster, crayfish
Molluscan shellfish, bivalvesFresh and frozenOysters, clams, mussels. Not scallop adductor muscle. Raw bivalves under the National Shellfish Sanitation Program are exempt.
  • Foods made with a listed food are covered too, if the listed ingredient stays in the same form, for example fresh tuna in a poke bowl.
  • Canned tuna is not one of the listed forms. Canning is a kill step, so the canner is exempt for that food if it keeps its receiving record and a record of the kill step.
  • The list changes. FDA announces changes in the Federal Register. Removals take effect at once; additions take effect 2 years after the notice (§ 1.1465).
03

Who must comply

Any "person" (individual, partnership, corporation or association) that manufactures, processes, packs or holds a listed food, at home or abroad. For tuna that usually means:

First land-based receivers

Docks, buyers and plants that take fish straight off a vessel. They assign the first lot code.

Processors

Loining, steaking, freezing, smoking, repacking or relabeling: each is a transformation with a new lot code.

Importers and distributors

Record receiving and shipping, keep the supplier's lot code, and pass it on.

Fish farms and ranches

Harvesting, cooling and initial packing apply to aquaculture. Farms also need a farm map in their plan.

Retailers and restaurants

Record receiving. Small ones ($250,000 or less a year) are exempt.

Not covered

Fishing vessel owners and operators (partly), transporters, nonprofits, and food for personal use.

Seafood supply chains often have 5 or more stops, and many partners still keep paper records. The rule only works if lot codes and KDEs travel with the fish, so agree early with suppliers and buyers how you'll exchange them.

04

Key terms

Critical Tracking Event (CTE)
An event in a food's supply chain: harvesting, cooling before initial packing, initial packing of a raw agricultural commodity, first land-based receiving of food from a fishing vessel, shipping, receiving or transformation.
Key Data Element (KDE)
A piece of information tied to a CTE that you must keep and/or pass on.
Traceability lot / lot code
A batch that has been initially packed, first received on land from a vessel, or transformed. The lot code is a descriptor, often letters and numbers, that uniquely identifies it in the records of the firm that assigned it.
Traceability lot code source
The place where the lot code was assigned.
Lot code source reference
An alternative way to point FDA to that place, such as an FDA Food Facility Registration Number or a web address that shows the location.
Location description
Business name, phone number, street address (or geographic coordinates), city, state and ZIP code, or the foreign equivalent including country.
Product description
Product name (with brand, commodity and variety if applicable), packaging size and packaging style. For seafood the name may be the species and/or acceptable market name.
Reference document
A business document that holds some of the KDEs: bills of lading, purchase orders, advance shipping notices, work orders, invoices, database records, batch logs, production logs, field tags, catch certificates, receipts.
First land-based receiver
The person taking possession of a food for the first time on land, directly from a fishing vessel.
Fishing vessel
Any vessel used for fishing or for helping other vessels fish, including supply, storage, refrigeration, transport or processing (Magnuson-Stevens Act definition).
Cooling
Active temperature reduction of a raw agricultural commodity by hydrocooling, icing, forced air, vacuum cooling or similar. Icing of seafood is excluded.
Initial packing
Packing a raw agricultural commodity, other than food from a fishing vessel, for the first time.
Transformation
Manufacturing, processing or changing a food (e.g. commingling, repacking, relabeling) or its packaging, when the output is a listed food. Not initial packing, or anything before it.
Shipping / receiving
Arranging transport from one location to another, and receiving after transport. Both include moves between two addresses of the same firm. Shipping excludes sales to consumers and donations of surplus food.
Commingled raw agricultural commodity
For fish from vessels: catch from different landing vessels mixed after the vessels have landed, before processing. Partly exempt.
Transporter
Someone who holds food only to move it. Transporters are exempt and are left out of location descriptions.
Point of contact
Someone who knows your traceability procedures: name and/or job title, and phone number.
05

Events and the data you keep

Each event has its own list of Key Data Elements. Wild-caught tuna usually goes through the first four; farmed tuna adds harvesting, cooling and initial packing.

Fishing vesselFirst land-based receivingShippingReceivingTransformationShippingReceiving at the store

First Land-Based Receiving

§ 1.1335 · The first firm to take the fish on land, straight from a fishing vessel
  1. Traceability lot code you assigned
  2. Species and/or acceptable market name (unpackaged), or product description (packaged)
  3. Quantity and unit of measure (e.g. 300 kg)
  4. Harvest date range and locations for the trip, using the NMFS Ocean Geographic Code, the FAO Major Fishing Area list or another widely recognized standard
  5. Location description for the first land-based receiver (the traceability lot code source), and the source reference if used
  6. Date the food was landed
  7. Reference document type and number

This is where wild-caught tuna gets its first lot code. The fishing vessel itself is exempt.

Shipping

§ 1.1340 · Anyone who arranges transport of the food to another location, including another site of the same firm
  1. Traceability lot code
  2. Quantity and unit of measure
  3. Product description
  4. Location description for the immediate subsequent recipient (not the transporter)
  5. Location description for where you shipped from
  6. Date shipped
  7. Location description for the traceability lot code source, or the source reference
  8. Reference document type and number (keep only)

Pass on: items 1 to 7 to the recipient, electronically, on paper or in any written form.

Not shipping: selling straight to consumers, or donating surplus food. Does not apply to farmed fish shipped before it is first packed.

Receiving

§ 1.1345(a) · Anyone who receives the food from another location, including an intracompany delivery
  1. Traceability lot code
  2. Quantity and unit of measure
  3. Product description
  4. Location description for the immediate previous source (not the transporter)
  5. Location description for where the food was received
  6. Date received
  7. Location description for the traceability lot code source, or the source reference
  8. Reference document type and number

Receiving from a firm that is exempt (§ 1.1345(b)): you assign the lot code yourself if none exists, and your receiving location becomes the lot code source. Restaurants and retailers don't assign one. Does not apply to the first land-based receiver.

Transformation

§ 1.1350 · Anyone who processes or changes the food: loining, steaking, freezing, smoking, commingling, repacking, relabeling
  1. For each input lot: its traceability lot code, product description and the quantity and unit used
  2. New traceability lot code
  3. Location description for where you transformed it (the lot code source), and the source reference if used
  4. Date transformation was completed
  5. Product description of the new food
  6. Quantity and unit of measure
  7. Reference document type and number

Farmed fish transformed before it was ever packed: also keep the Initial Packing details. Retailers and restaurants don't record transformation of food they sell to consumers.

Harvesting

§ 1.1325(a) · Fish farms (aquaculture, tuna ranching)
  1. Location description for the immediate subsequent recipient (not the transporter)
  2. Commodity and, if applicable, variety
  3. Quantity and unit of measure
  4. Location description for the farm
  5. Name of the container (pond, pool, tank, cage) the fish came from, matching the farmer's own name, or something at least as precise
  6. Date of harvesting
  7. Reference document type and number

Pass on: your business name, phone number and items 1 to 6 to the initial packer.

Wild-caught tuna has no harvesting event; the first land-based receiver records the catch.

Cooling (before initial packing)

§ 1.1325(b) · Fish farms or coolers, before the fish is first packed
  1. Location description for the immediate subsequent recipient
  2. Commodity and, if applicable, variety
  3. Quantity and unit of measure
  4. Location description for where you cooled the food
  5. Date of cooling
  6. Location description for the farm
  7. Reference document type and number

Pass on: items 1 to 6 to the initial packer.

Cooling means active temperature reduction, such as hydrocooling or forced air. Icing seafood is not cooling.

Initial Packing

§ 1.1330(a) · Whoever first packs farmed fish
  1. Commodity and variety received; date received; quantity and unit received
  2. Location description for the farm, and the container name
  3. Business name and phone number of the harvester; date of harvesting
  4. Where and when it was cooled (if it was)
  5. Traceability lot code you assigned
  6. Product description, quantity and unit of the packed food
  7. Location description for where you packed it (the lot code source), and the source reference if used
  8. Date of initial packing
  9. Reference document type and number

From an exempt farm (§ 1.1330(c)): record who you received it from in place of the farm, container and harvester details. Sprouts have extra seed details (§ 1.1330(b)), not relevant to tuna.

06

Traceability lot codes

You assign a traceability lot code only when you (§ 1.1320):

Initially pack

farmed fish

First receive on land

fish from a vessel

Transform

any listed food

Receive from an exempt firm

if it has no code yet

  • Never create a new code when you only ship, receive or store. Keep the code you were given and pass it on.
  • The format is up to you, as long as it identifies one lot uniquely in your records. Many firms use prefix + date + running number.
  • The lot code source travels with the code, so the next firm knows where it was assigned. A source reference (such as an FDA registration number) can stand in for the address.
07

The traceability plan

Every covered firm must keep a written plan (§ 1.1315) containing:

  1. Record procedures, including the format and location of your records.
  2. How you identify foods on the list that you handle.
  3. How you assign traceability lot codes, if you do.
  4. A point of contact for questions about the plan and records.
  5. A farm map if you grow or raise a listed food: for aquaculture, the name and location of each pond, pool, tank or cage, with geographic coordinates.

Update the plan whenever your practices change, and keep each previous version for 2 years after you replace it. Step 2 of this tool writes a first draft from your answers, saves versions and prints them.

08

Keeping records and answering FDA

Form

Original paper or electronic records, or true copies (photocopies, pictures, scans). Electronic records may link to the information. Keep them legible and safe from loss.

How long

2 years from the date you created or obtained them. Previous traceability plans: 2 years after you replace them.

Where

Off-site storage is fine, and another firm may keep them for you, if they can be produced within 24 hours. You can use records you already keep, and more than one set, as long as your plan says where they are.

When FDA asks

Within 24 hours, or a reasonable time FDA agrees to, with anything needed to read them: coding systems, glossaries, abbreviations. A request can come in person or by phone.

Sortable spreadsheet

When FDA asks to prevent or mitigate an outbreak, assist a recall or address another public health threat, CTE records go in an electronic sortable spreadsheet for the foods, dates or lot codes it names.

Small-firm waiver

Farms with $250,000 or less in yearly sales, and other firms with $1 million or less, may answer in another form. Records must still be kept.

Language

Records may be in any language. FDA can ask for an English translation within a reasonable time.

Part 11

Electronic records kept only for this rule are exempt from FDA's electronic-records rule (21 CFR Part 11).

10

If you don't comply

Prohibited act

Breaking a record-keeping requirement of the rule is a prohibited act under section 301(e) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (except for farms). That can lead to civil or criminal penalties.

Imports refused

Food offered for import can be refused admission under section 801(a)(4) if it appears the record-keeping requirements were not met.

Frequently asked questions

When do I have to comply?

FDA won't enforce the rule before 20 July 2028. The rule itself took effect in January 2023, and its original compliance date was 20 January 2026.

Do I need special software?

No. Paper and electronic records are both allowed. But FDA can ask for an electronic sortable spreadsheet within 24 hours, which is hard to build by hand for a busy operation. Smaller firms ($1 million or less in food sales, $250,000 for farms) may answer in another form.

Who assigns the lot code for wild-caught tuna?

The first land-based receiver: whoever takes the fish on land straight from the vessel. The vessel itself is exempt.

Does icing fish on the boat or at the dock count as cooling?

No. The rule's definition of cooling excludes icing of seafood. Cooling events only apply to farmed fish cooled before initial packing.

Is canned tuna covered?

Canned tuna isn't one of the listed forms. The canner applies a kill step, so it is exempt for that food, but it must keep its receiving record for the fish and a record of the kill step. A supplier that sells fish to a canner can be exempt too, with a written agreement renewed every 3 years.

Do I need a new lot code when I repack or relabel?

Repacking and relabeling are part of the rule's definition of transformation, and transforming a food is one of the times you must assign a lot code. Only shipping, receiving or storing never gets a new code.

What do I send my customer with each shipment?

Items 1 to 7 of your shipping record: lot code, quantity and unit, product description, the recipient's and your shipping location, ship date, and the lot code source or its reference. Any written form works: an advance shipping notice, the bill of lading, a label or an EDI message. Use "Copy for the recipient" on a shipping record.

I import tuna. What do I record?

Receiving, with the supplier's lot code and lot code source, then shipping when you send it on. Foreign firms are covered by the rule too, and food can be refused entry if the records aren't kept.

How long do I keep records?

2 years from the date you created or obtained them. Earlier versions of your traceability plan: 2 years after you replace them.

Can someone else keep the records for me?

Yes. Another firm, or a software provider, can keep them on your behalf, but you remain responsible for producing them within 24 hours.

Is this the same as NOAA's SIMP?

No. SIMP (NOAA) checks that imported fish was caught legally. FSMA 204 (FDA) is about food safety: tracing where a lot went, fast. Some details overlap, but they are separate records for separate agencies.

Does this tool send anything to FDA?

No. It keeps your records and builds the spreadsheet; you decide when and how to send it.
